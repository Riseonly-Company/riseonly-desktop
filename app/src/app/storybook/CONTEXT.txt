================================================================================
app/src/app/storybook — the Phase 5 gate, made visible
================================================================================

READ FIRST
  ../../../../CONTEXT.txt   workspace rules, boundaries, commands
  ../../../../PHASES.txt    "PHASE 5 — DESIGN SYSTEM", and its GATE

WHAT THIS IS

  The gate says: every component in a storybook, light and dark, three density
  multipliers, no literal colour or length outside rise-theme. This directory is
  the first half. The second half is mechanical and lives in
  scripts/check-design-tokens.py, which runs inside `cargo make lint`.

    cargo make dev-macos --design

  Underneath it is still a launch argument, not a route. The reference gates its
  own developer surfaces the same way (-riseChatPerformanceTools), and a
  RootRoute variant would put a developer screen inside the product's exhaustive
  route match forever.

  --design is translated to the app's -riseStorybook by scripts/dev-args.sh,
  which every dev-* task sources. Put friendly names for launch arguments there
  rather than in one task's script: two tasks with two spellings is how the
  Linux one silently stops accepting a flag the macOS one takes. A test in
  storybook.rs reads that script back, so renaming LAUNCH_ARGUMENT without
  editing it fails the build instead of quietly opening the product.

WHY IT BUILDS ITS OWN THEMES

  Six columns — two appearances by three densities — stand side by side in one
  window, each rendered from an AppTheme built locally rather than from the
  installed global. A difference between light and dark then becomes something
  you can see rather than something you have to remember across a restart.

  This is also why every component in rise-ui takes `&AppTheme` explicitly
  instead of reaching for the global itself. Keep it that way: a component that
  reads the global can only ever be shown once.

WHAT IT DELIBERATELY DOES NOT SHOW

  The native tier of a material. GlassPanel resolves to an AppKit view or to
  paint depending on the machine, and only the painted tier is a drawing this
  window can place six times. The storybook shows the painted form and says so;
  judging real glass means running the app.

  Every icon. 229 SF Symbols mapped to Lucide is a stress test, not a review.
  The sample is small on purpose, and the 26 whose match is a compromise are
  listed in assets/icons/sf-to-lucide.json under "approximate" — those are the
  ones a designer has to sign off.

WHAT IT MUST NEVER DO

  Ship in a release build's UI without an explicit flag. Performance and
  developer surfaces do not appear on their own in staging or production; that
  rule is the reference's and it carries over.
