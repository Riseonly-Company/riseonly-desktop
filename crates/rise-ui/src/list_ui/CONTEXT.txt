================================================================================
rise-ui/list_ui — long lists and their edges
================================================================================

  list_ui.rs     the wrapper over gpui's `list`, plus a velocity estimate.
  pagination.rs  the trigger policy. Pure, and the interesting part.

WHAT IS NOT PORTED, AND WHY

  PHASES.txt Phase 1 settled it: gpui's `list` is a SumTree with variable row
  heights, ListAlignment::Bottom and splice-based prepend anchoring, so most of
  the reference's RiseManualListView does not need porting. Wrap it; do not
  reimplement it.

THE ONE THING THAT COULD NOT BE INHERITED

  The reference triggers pagination on a ROW INDEX: `index >= itemCount - 6`.
  Six rows is about a viewport on a phone and a third of one on a desktop window
  the user has dragged tall, so the same rule fires far too late on the machine
  this runs on. The desktop rule is DISTANCE, and the CLAUDE.md says so.

  threshold = clamp(one viewport + |velocity| x 0.6s, 400pt, 2400pt)

  The viewport term covers a list at rest or scrolled slowly. The velocity term
  covers a fling, where a screen of lead time is a fraction of a second. Both are
  clamped so a very tall window does not prefetch the whole timeline and a very
  fast fling does not either.

  Direction matters: an edge is armed when the list is MOVING TOWARDS it, or when
  it is already within the floor distance. Without the direction test a fast
  scroll upwards fetches the bottom too, purely because the bottom happens to be
  within a fling's reach.

WHERE THE VELOCITY COMES FROM

  Two samples and a clock. gpui's scroll event carries a visible RANGE and no
  velocity, and the phone's `predictedEndLocation` has no counterpart here at
  all. Smoothed, floored at one frame's interval — two callbacks in the same
  millisecond would otherwise report tens of thousands of points a second and pin
  the threshold at its ceiling — and reset to zero after a quarter second of
  quiet.

AN UNSIZED LIST IS AN INVISIBLE LIST

  gpui's `list` defaults to ListSizingBehavior::Auto, which asks taffy for
  `height: auto`, and it keeps its rows OUT of the layout tree — it paints them
  itself in prepaint, inside whatever bounds it was given. So a list with no
  height of its own measures to zero and paints nothing, however many rows it
  holds, and nothing anywhere reports an error: the screen just looks empty.
  Wrapping it in a `div().size_full()` does not help, because that div is the
  PARENT and the list still asks for `auto`.

  `element()` therefore returns the list already `.size_full()`, which is what
  Zed's own call sites do. A caller only has to give it a container with a
  definite height — `ScreenShellUi` does, via `flex_1().min_h_0()`.

  tests/list_ui_fills_its_screen.rs stands the real screen geometry up in a real
  window and asserts the viewport was measured, because nothing short of a drawn
  window can tell the difference.

ROW IDENTITY IS THE CALLER'S JOB

  gpui keys rows by index inside the tree, so a splice at the wrong place reuses
  the wrong row's state. That shows up as a like landing on the post above.

NOTHING MAY ASK THE LIST WHERE IT IS FROM INSIDE ITS OWN CALLBACKS

  gpui holds `ListState`'s `RefCell` mutably for the WHOLE of the scroll handler
  (`fn scroll` -> `handler(..)`) and for the whole of the row-render closure
  (`prepaint` -> `layout_items`). Calling `scroll_px_offset_for_scrollbar`,
  `viewport_bounds`, `max_offset_for_scrollbar`, `item_count` or `splice` from
  either one is "RefCell already mutably borrowed" — and because the callback is
  reached through an Objective-C frame, that panic cannot unwind and the process
  ABORTS instead of showing a backtrace. It took the app down on the first
  two-finger scroll of a feed that finally had rows in it.

  `on_edge` therefore reads NOTHING in the handler: it takes `cx.defer` and does
  the whole distance policy there. `defer` and not `on_next_frame` — gpui runs
  next-frame callbacks inside an update of the ROOT view, which is a second
  borrow of whatever screen owns the list.

  `ListScrollEvent` carries `visible_range` and `count` and is safe to read; it
  just has no pixels in it, which is why the policy waits a beat instead.
